GigHub

A gateway to the vibrant local music scene

My Role

Primary UX Designer

Responsibilities

User research, Conducted interviews, Evaluated research insights, Wireframing, Interface design

Tools

Figma, Adobe Photoshop, Google Forms

Context

Project for CreateRU, a designathon hosted by Rutgers’ design club

The Challenge: Design for your local community

The challenge we were given was “design for your local community.” As we were currently living at Rutgers University in New Brunswick, NJ, we brainstormed aspects of life here that could be improved, both for students and those who lived in the area.

We eventually landed upon an area that had big opportunities, yet lacked a lot of support: the underground music scene.

The Problem

New Brunswick, NJ has a very vibrant underground music scene. There are countless bands and artists in the area always creating new music. As a member of a band myself, I can see how music production and creation has gotten as accessible as ever, and that music is created all the time. Some even do gigs at local restaurants, or independent shows. There is a huge amount of music and musicians in the area, most of who would love to share their art with others, but may lack the means to do so.

On the other hand, there is a huge population of people looking to have fun on the weekends, be social, and listen to new music. Tens of thousands of college students live at Rutgers at any given moment, and, of course, would love to have something fun to do on a Friday night.

However, there is a disconnect between these two parties. The underground scene can be hard to explore. There is no simple way for students and other locals to receive information regarding the location and times for shows and gigs, as well as who will be playing. It is also difficult for bands to gain recognition and find people to come to shows.

We saw a great opportunity here - an opportunity to connect these two parties. A way to get bands and musicians the audience and shows they dream of, while giving everyone else a great way to spend weekend nights out, allowing them to meet new people and discover new music.

The Solution

We decided to design an app to serve as a hub for the local music scene, which can help users find shows to enjoy while giving artists exposure and a following. We wanted the app to be able to:

  • Show users upcoming gigs and shows in the area
  • Serve as platform for bands to get their name out there, showcasing their information and shows
  • Allow users to follow artists and keep up to date on their favorite artists' shows

Identifying User Needs

As the drummer in a band myself, I was in a good position to understand a lot of the goals users would have when using an app like this. First, I talked with my fellow band members to come to conclusions on the problems we faced the most often. Then, I interviewed members of other bands to see if they shared our hardships and to learn about their daily lives as band members. Finally, I interviewed local students to find out what features would benefit them most, and what exactly they would be looking to achieve in an app to connect them to the local music scene.

Here are some important moments in our conversations:

To summarize, students really liked the idea of an app that could show them shows to attend, as well as expose them to new music. Currently, they don’t know much about the local music scene but wanted to learn more.

Also, band members would find this very helpful as it is a good way to get recognition and an audience at gigs and other shows. They stated that a platform like this is nonexistent at the moment and it would be very beneficial to have all of their information in one place. They also discussed playing gigs at restaurants and bars, which is a goal of some bands. But they would have difficulty showing their credibility without a following of some kind, which makes it hard for establishments to take them seriously. Especially since these establishments would hope to attract more customers with live music. With this app, this is no longer something that needs to be worried about.

So, all in all, this is a win-win-win.

It’s a win for the average person, as they can find gigs and shows to attend, and a fun way to go out, be social, and discover new music.

It’s a win for restaurants and similar establishments, as they can have these gigs be advertised by the musicians on this app, which would attract a crowd, therefore more customers, and more business.

It’s a win for bands and musicians, as they can get their name out there, and have people discover their music and attend their shows and gigs. This platform could even give them enough of a following for establishments like restaurants or bars to consider having them play gigs.

Finally, we decided on the name GigHub, a simple and catchy name to describe the app that serves as the hub for local gigs.

The Design

After a few iterations on designs, with musicians and students testing and giving feedback on prototypes, we were able to finish with these final designs.

Keep up with favorite artists

The home screen is where users can keep up to date with their favorite artists. This screen allows users to see the gigs they are most interested in at a glance, and view profiles of musicians they follow.

Gig locations are split between 3 categories: restaurants, bars/clubs, and shows. This gives a quick glimpse into what kind of experience the event could be. For example, someone looking for a more relaxed night out with music in the background could opt for a gig at a restaurant. Someone who wants to focus completely on the music and the bands playing might choose to look for independent shows to attend.

Find new gigs nearby

The explore screen shows users all gigs nearby, from soonest to latest. This page makes it very easy to feel out the local scene, and see what kinds of musicians are nearby.

Save gigs for future plans

The liked gigs screen lets users easily see all the gigs they liked, to keep track of ones they would be interested in going to.

Learn about local artists

Tapping on an artist's name or profile picture brings the user to their profile. Their profile page serves as a promotional platform for each artist, and it contains a bio, pictures, and their upcoming gigs. The artists' social media links are also provided so users can explore more about the artist and listen to their music if they've posted it anywhere.

Plan the fun night out

Tapping on a gig in a list will bring the user to the gig info page, where the user can learn all they need to know about the event. Tapping on the name of the establishment will redirect the user to their maps app for easy directions. I also wanted there to be a way to gauge how big of a crowd there will be at each gig, so the “interested” button can be used to let the band, venue, and other people know that you may be coming, and overall, so everyone can see how popular it will be.
