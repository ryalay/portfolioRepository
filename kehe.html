KeHE Distributors

UI/UX Design Intern

At KeHE Distributors, I was a member of the UX team working on various projects with the teams focused on the enterprise and retailer platforms.


KeHE is a whole foods distribution company that manages the flow of goods from suppliers to retailers. They work with over 8,500 brands (like Kind bars, Blue Diamond almonds), and distribute foods to over 30,000 retailers (like Publix, Walmart).

At my time with KeHE, I worked with another designer in designing various webpages for the enterprise platform. This is the platform where the sales team can manage shipments to retailers from KeHE’s warehouses.


My responsibilities at KeHE were a unique challenge for me. Until this point in my design career, I had been focusing on consumer-facing interfaces that people like me would use. Now, it was my job to design interfaces for professionals within the company that needed to accomplish very specific tasks.

The nature of this company meant that these interfaces convey a lot of information on a single page. For example, on the manage shipments pages, the shipments to retailers need to be clearly listed out, showing the user information like shipment number, the distribution center, cost of the order, and much more. Designing interfaces that contained so much information and niche features, while still being user centered and easy to understand proved to be a challenge, but I persevered, and learned so much from this experience, while my skills improved drastically.



Enterprise Designs

We were tasked by the product owner to create a feature that would allow a customer service representative to delete multiple specific items from multiple shipments. In case retailers no longer wanted specific items, but they were arriving in multiple different shipments, this feature would allow quick searching and deletion of all of them. We decided to implement this feature into the manage shipments page.

A very specific feature like this required a lot of brainstorming, and we came up with multiple ways to solve this problem.



The first flow we tested was a modal which guided the user through a bulk delete wizard, where first the items are searched and then the shipments containing those items appear. Then, the user can then pick and choose what to delete from the shipments.

The next flow involves filtering shipments that contain the specific items in question through the filters menu. From there, all the shipments containing these items are shown, and then, the user can select the shipments which the item needs to be deleted from, and choose which items in each need to be deleted.
